Experiment logs are most useful when they make comparison cheap. The goal is not to record everything. The goal is to preserve the decisions that explain a result.

## Minimum useful fields

Each run should record the hypothesis, dataset version, configuration, metric target, and a short interpretation. The interpretation matters because metrics alone rarely explain what changed.

## Compare by question

Instead of organizing experiments only by date, group them by the question they answer:

- Does the new feature improve validation performance?
- Does the simpler model train faster without losing accuracy?
- Does the policy remain stable under a harder workload?

This keeps the log aligned with the research process rather than the file system.
