Good writing systems do not need many moving parts. They need clear entry points, stable review habits, and a low-friction path from rough notes to published work.

## Capture

Capture should be fast enough that it does not become a separate task. I keep short notes close to the work: experiment logs near code, product observations near screenshots, and article fragments near the outline.

The important distinction is between *capture* and *commitment*. A captured idea is allowed to be incomplete. A committed note has a title, a purpose, and a likely audience.

## Shape

Every durable note gets three passes:

1. What problem is this note actually solving?
2. What would a reader need to know before the conclusion makes sense?
3. What should be removed because it only preserves my thinking history?

That last question is the highest-leverage edit. Published writing should preserve the argument, not every step that produced it.

## Publish

Publishing regularly is easier when the site has a narrow surface area. This blog keeps posts in MDX, projects in content collections, and deployment in GitHub Actions. There is no database and no runtime service to maintain.

The result is intentionally boring infrastructure: static files, versioned content, and a repeatable build.
